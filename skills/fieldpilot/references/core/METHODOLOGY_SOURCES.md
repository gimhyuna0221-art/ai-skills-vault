# FieldPilot Methodology Sources

Compact, paraphrased reference. FieldPilot may cite these principles in
plain language when a recommendation is genuinely supported — see
"Source-use rule" at the bottom of this file for exactly how to phrase
single-source vs. multi-source support, and how to phrase across source
classes. Do not copy long passages from the originals; only the
paraphrased principle and the "Safe to infer" / "Do not infer" lines
below are for use inside FieldPilot. Every citation in this file was
checked against the publisher's own record (title, authors, journal,
year, volume/pages) before being kept — none are trusted from memory.

## Source classes

- **A — Direct domain peer-reviewed evidence**: entrepreneurship,
  startup experimentation, marketing, pricing/WTP research. Most
  directly transferable to FieldPilot's actual subject matter.
- **B — General research-method peer-reviewed evidence**: feasibility
  studies, qualitative/mixed-methods guidance, validity. Often written
  for clinical/RCT feasibility research — the *structural/procedural*
  principle transfers to B2B market validation, but domain-specific
  numbers, thresholds, and clinical machinery do not.
- **C — Public authoritative guidance**: GOV.UK, NICE, DfE, UK
  Government. Practical, tested guidance, not peer-reviewed research.
- **D — Reputable practitioner guidance**: e.g. Nielsen Norman Group.
  May support logistics/wording; must never override A/B/C or be
  presented as equally authoritative.
- **E — FieldPilot-native practical frameworks**: FieldPilot's own
  operational syntheses, informed by A–D but not themselves a
  published, directly-validated framework. See the "E" section below
  for the exhaustive list — never call these "연구로 증명된
  프레임워크" (research-proven framework).

A, B, C, and D are not interchangeable. Do not present a class-D
opinion with class-A/B/C authority, and do not present a class-B
clinical-feasibility finding as class-A startup evidence.

## A — Direct domain peer-reviewed evidence

### A1. Camuffo A, Cordova A, Gambardella A, Spina C. "A Scientific
Approach to Entrepreneurial Decision Making: Evidence from a
Randomized Control Trial." *Management Science*. 2019 (online),
2020;66(2):564–586. DOI: 10.1287/mnsc.2018.3249
Verified: title/authors/journal/volume/pages confirmed against the
publisher record (INFORMS PubsOnline).
Findings: in an RCT with 116 Italian startups, founders trained to use
explicit hypotheses and rigorous market tests made more precise
go/no-go calls (fewer false positives, more recovered false
negatives), were more likely to pivot on evidence, and did not drop
out more than the control group.
Safe to infer: explicit hypotheses and rigorous market tests can
improve entrepreneurial decision precision; evidence should be allowed
to update or reverse a founder's prior belief rather than only
confirm it.
Do not infer: that FieldPilot's exact workflow/stage structure was
itself experimentally tested, or that every startup must use one
identical experimental design — the study tested a general skill
(hypothesis-driven testing), not a specific tool or stage sequence.

### A2. Ghezzi A. "Digital startups and the adoption and implementation
of Lean Startup Approaches: Effectuation, Bricolage and Opportunity
Creation in practice." *Technological Forecasting and Social Change*.
2019;146:945–960. DOI: 10.1016/j.techfore.2018.09.017
Verified: title/journal/volume/pages confirmed via publisher/aggregator
records (ScienceDirect, RePEc/IDEAS).
Findings: a large-scale study of how digital startups actually adopt
and implement Lean Startup practices (Customer Development + Lean
Startup), framed against Effectuation/Bricolage/Opportunity-Creation
theory; documents real operational difficulties founders face
(identifying trial users, setting testing priorities, designing
experiments) and iterative pivoting in practice.
Safe to infer: customer-development/experimentation practices are
genuinely used — and genuinely hard to execute — in real digital
startups; the specific frictions FieldPilot's "운영 현실성 점검"
addresses (finding accessible test users, prioritizing what to test)
match documented real-world difficulty, not a hypothetical concern.
Do not infer: that Lean Startup is proven universally superior to
alternative approaches, or that this study's descriptive/qualitative
findings establish causal superiority of any specific method.

### A3. Miller KM, Hofstetter R, Krohmer H, Zhang ZJ. "How Should
Consumers' Willingness to Pay be Measured? An Empirical Comparison of
State-of-the-Art Approaches." *Journal of Marketing Research*.
2011;48(1):172–184. DOI: 10.1509/jmkr.48.1.172
Verified: title/authors/journal/volume/issue/pages confirmed against
the publisher record (SAGE/journals.sagepub.com).
Findings: compared four WTP-elicitation methods (open-ended questions,
choice-based conjoint, BDM mechanism, incentive-aligned conjoint)
against real purchase behavior; respondents were more price-sensitive
under incentive-aligned/real conditions than under non-incentive-aligned
stated-preference conditions — but even the weaker (hypothetical-bias-
prone) methods could still yield roughly the right demand curve for
some decisions.
Safe to infer: stated/hypothetical WTP and real purchase behavior are
measurably different things — SKILL.md's rule that a stated WTP
response ("$10 내겠다") is not itself a payment is directly supported.
How WTP is elicited (open-ended vs. incentive-aligned vs. real
transaction) materially changes how much the answer can be trusted.
Do not infer: that stated WTP is useless (the paper shows it can still
be informative for some decisions), or that one specific elicitation
method is universally correct for every FieldPilot project — the
right method depends on what decision it needs to inform.

## B — General research-method peer-reviewed evidence

*Domain note: all three papers below are written for clinical/RCT
pilot-feasibility research. Only the structural/procedural principle
transfers to general B2B market validation — sample-size numbers,
statistical thresholds, and RCT-specific machinery do NOT.*

### B1. Mellor K, Albury C, Dutton SJ, Eldridge S, Hopewell S.
"Recommendations for progression criteria during external randomised
pilot trial design, conduct, analysis and reporting." *Pilot and
Feasibility Studies*. 2023;9:59. DOI: 10.1186/s40814-023-01291-5
Verified: title/authors/journal/volume/article number confirmed via
PMC (PMC10105402) and the publisher record.
Findings: progression criteria should map to each feasibility
objective (not just recruitment/retention); numeric and non-numeric
("qualitative") criteria can be combined; numeric targets need a
stated rationale, which "need not be statistical as pilot trials are
usually underpowered for hypothesis testing"; a Red-Amber-Green
guideline-style judgement often works better than a rigid pass/fail
rule; small pilot sample sizes mean rate estimates carry considerable
uncertainty.
Safe to infer: numeric thresholds always need a stated justification
(matches FieldPilot's "숫자 추천 Guard"); a qualitative/guideline-style
judgment criterion is a valid, non-corner-cutting default for a small
pilot — do not force a numeric pass/fail rule where none is justified.
Do not infer: any specific sample size, statistical power target, or
RAG threshold value from clinical pilot-trial practice as a B2B
market-validation rule — only the *structural* principle (map
criteria to objectives; justify numbers; guidelines over rigid rules;
small samples carry uncertainty) transfers.

### B2. Aschbrenner KA, Kruse G, Gallo JJ, Plano Clark VL. "Applying
mixed methods to pilot feasibility studies to inform intervention
trials." *Pilot and Feasibility Studies*. 2022;8:217. DOI:
10.1186/s40814-022-01178-x
Verified: title/authors/journal/volume/article number confirmed via
PMC (PMC9511762) and the publisher record.
Findings: investigators should formulate explicit "why/what/when/how"
mixed-methods integration questions before picking methods, rather
than defaulting to a "usual" method (e.g. focus groups) without
considering alternatives; data collection should be bounded by what's
needed to answer the feasibility questions and progression criteria,
not maximized across every possible stakeholder; quantitative and
qualitative results should be compared explicitly (e.g. via joint
displays), treating disagreement as a source of insight, not a
problem to suppress.
Safe to infer: FieldPilot should align the evidence source with the
current question rather than defaulting automatically to interviews;
it should not gather more data than the current decision needs;
pairing a count (e.g. "1/5 used it") with the qualitative reason why
is expected good practice, not extra work; contradictory
quantitative/qualitative signals should be reported, not hidden.
Do not infer: that formal mixed-methods integration (joint displays,
etc.) is required for every startup question, or that this paper
prescribes a universal startup experiment ordering.

### B3. O'Cathain A, Hoddinott P, Lewin S, Thomas K, Young B, Adamson
J, Jansen Y, Mills N, Moore G, Donovan J. "Maximising the impact of
qualitative research in feasibility studies for randomised controlled
trials: guidance for researchers." *Pilot and Feasibility Studies*.
2015;1:32. DOI: 10.1186/s40814-015-0026-y
Verified: title/authors/journal/volume/article number confirmed via
PMC (PMC5154038) and the publisher record. A conference abstract of
the same guidance by the same authors was also presented at the 3rd
International Clinical Trials Methodology Conference and indexed
separately as *Trials* 2015;16(Suppl 2):O88, DOI:
10.1186/1745-6215-16-S2-O88 — this is the SAME underlying guidance
(same authors, same 16 items/5 domains), not a second independent
source, and is not counted as one for the 2-source consensus rule
below.
Findings (16 items, 5 domains): prioritise initial questions by
identifying key uncertainties, while allowing emergent questions;
search existing systematic reviews (or, failing that, studies of
similar interventions) before new primary research; base method
selection on the research question and context, not habit; diversity
across participants/sites/timing can matter more than raw sample size;
qualitative sample sizes in feasibility studies are "typically between
5 and 20 individuals" (explicitly a clinical-feasibility-study figure,
citing separate usability-testing research for the 10-user/80%-problem
and 20-user/95%-problem figures); small/early findings should be
interpreted cautiously and reported as such.
Safe to infer: prioritise the most important uncertainty; check
existing evidence before commissioning new primary research; pick the
method for the question and context, not by default; diversity/context
can matter more than raw participant count; this literature's general
caution about interpreting small samples cautiously is consistent with
FieldPilot's own "표본 해석" rule — but that rule's specific claim (a
single observed success can support feasibility-in-context but not
market demand) is a FieldPilot-native synthesis (class E), not
something this paper itself establishes; this paper does not validate
one observation as sufficient evidence of anything, nor does it define
a feasibility-vs-market-demand distinction.
Do not infer: the "5–20" figure, or the usability-testing "10 users /
20 users" figures cited inside this paper, as a FieldPilot sample-size
rule for B2B market validation — these are stated for clinical
qualitative feasibility research and a different (usability-testing)
literature respectively, and FieldPilot's "숫자 추천 Guard" already
forbids inventing a fixed headcount by default.

## C — Public authoritative guidance

### C1. GOV.UK Service Manual — "Plan user research for your service"
https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service
Principles: prioritise research questions; select methods based on
what must be learned; seek strong/reliable evidence for the least
time/effort/cost; prefer simple methods for inexperienced teams; run
iterative rounds rather than one huge round; sample size depends on
method.
Safe to infer: small, sequential, method-appropriate rounds are a
legitimate default for a novice-run pilot — not a corner-cut.
Do not infer: any specific participant-count figure from this guidance
as a universal FieldPilot market-validation sample size — it states
sample size "depends on method" and gives no fixed number to import.

### C2. GOV.UK Service Manual — "Analyse a research session"
https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/analyse-a-research-session
Principles: separate observation from interpretation; cluster
observations into findings; turn findings into actions; analyse soon
after research; preserve verbatim user evidence where useful.
Safe to infer: FieldPilot's CONFIRMED OBSERVATION vs. MODEL
INTERPRETATION split and its 관찰→발견→함의→행동 structure are
directly supported.

### C3. GOV.UK Service Manual — "User research in discovery"
https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/user-research-in-discovery
Principles: existing data, previous research, and back-office/support
evidence are legitimate research inputs alongside new observation and
interviews — interviews are not the only valid source.
Safe to infer: FieldPilot's "증거 확보 사다리" starting with existing
internal evidence and desk/external evidence before new primary
research is consistent with this guidance.

### C4. Department for Education (Digital) — "Desk Research in
Discovery" https://dfedigital.blog.gov.uk/2023/12/20/desk-research-in-discovery/
Principles: desk research helps establish orientation and prepare
later research (forums, social media, analytics, prior research can
all help); you often cannot know whether desk-research data actually
represents your own users, so assumptions drawn from it need follow-up
research.
Safe to infer: desk research is a legitimate, sometimes-first step,
but it narrows/orients hypotheses rather than confirming them for the
user's specific target segment — matches SKILL.md's desk-research
CAN/CANNOT split exactly.
Do not infer: that desk research alone validates demand for the
user's exact target segment.

### C5. NICE Real-world evidence framework — Appendix 4 (conduct of
qualitative research studies)
https://www.nice.org.uk/corporate/ecd9/chapter/appendix-4-conduct-of-qualitative-research-studies
Principles: methods must fit the research aim; consider context;
explain sampling/data collection/analysis; investigate negative cases;
support findings with collected data; preserve methodological
limitations; consider triangulation/credibility; evidence from one
population/setting does not automatically generalize to another
target population/setting (external validity).
Safe to infer: negative/contradicting evidence must stay visible, and
limitations belong in the final report, not just the raw log. External
evidence (desk research, a competitor, a founder anecdote, a news
article) needs an explicit similarity check (target user, setting,
geography, business size/type, workflow, recency) before it is
transferred to the user's own market — SKILL.md's "외부 근거 기록"
rule implements this directly.
Do not infer: any healthcare-specific causal-inference or statistical-
adjustment machinery (e.g. propensity scoring, GRADE certainty
ratings) into ordinary B2B market-validation reasoning — only the
external-validity/triangulation *principle* transfers.

### C6. UK Government guidance on testing/piloting services
Principles: tie pilot purpose to success criteria; capture
internal/external factors affecting delivery; final report should
compare results against objectives; identify gaps, changes needed, and
next-stage decisions.
Safe to infer: the *content* of FieldPilot's final report (purpose vs.
criteria, delivery factors, comparison against objectives, gaps and
next-stage decisions) follows this reporting shape.
Do not infer: that this source mandates FieldPilot's specific decision
to split that content into exactly three files (시장검증_결과요약 /
근거와_한계 / 다음실험_실행계획) — that split is a pragmatic
product-format choice (see class E), or that government
procurement/service-pilot rules are startup market-demand standards.

## D — Reputable practitioner guidance

May include: Nielsen Norman Group pilot-testing/user-research guidance;
reputable UX research reporting guidance; other high-quality B2B/
customer-discovery material. Class D must never override a class A/B/C
principle or be presented as equally authoritative — label it as
practitioner guidance, not peer-reviewed research or public
authoritative guidance, if the distinction matters to the
recommendation. Practitioner guidance may support logistics/wording
(e.g. how to phrase an interview question) but must not be the basis
for a strong evidentiary claim.

## E — FieldPilot-native practical frameworks

The following are FieldPilot's own operational syntheses, informed by
the A–D principles above but NOT themselves an exact framework that
any cited publication directly evaluated: 증거 확보 사다리 (Evidence
Acquisition Ladder), the 10-stage 단계 라우팅 workflow, the
Navigational HARD GATE, the specific CONFIRMED/PROPOSED/INFERRED/
METHOD_CONFLICT provenance implementation, Alternative Map categories,
장벽 구분 (barrier taxonomy), 실행 키트 (Execution Kit) structure, the
final 3-file package, recommendation-first novice UX, artifact
routing/naming, and the "표본 해석" rule's specific claim that a
single observed success can support feasibility-in-context but not
market demand (general caution about small-sample interpretation is
class-B-supported per B3; this specific feasibility-vs-market-demand
framing is FieldPilot's own synthesis, not something B3 itself
establishes).

Describe these to a user only as "FieldPilot practical framework
informed by the research principles above" (or the plain-Korean
equivalent). Never say "research-proven FieldPilot framework" or
imply a cited source directly validated the exact structure — unless
that exact structure was genuinely evaluated by a cited study, which
is not currently the case for any item in this list.

## V1.5 S-* source registry (runtime resolution)

These `S-*` IDs are what `EXPERT_CORE_RULES.md`, the method cards, and
the domain adapters cite at runtime. This section is the runtime
source-resolution table for that identifier scheme, ported faithfully
from `V1.5_FINAL_EXPERT_CORE_SPEC.md` Section 24 (the frozen spec is
the source of truth; this section must be kept in sync with it, not
edited independently). A rule that cites an `S-*` ID with no matching
row below is unresolvable and MUST fail closed for the affected expert
claim rather than be asserted unsupported.

The Class column is the `RuleSourceLink` relationship to the rule
family the source is listed for — it is neither a permanent
publication label nor a quality score. The "Safe to use for / do not
infer" column is the scope boundary that prevents claim overreach and
MUST be preserved and enforced verbatim; it is not decorative text.

### Source-registry maintenance (ported from spec Section 24.5)

Before implementation/release, each source record MUST add publication
metadata, edition/version, access date, funding/conflict,
original-versus-secondary access, active rule IDs, and archived
locator where licensing permits. At each later methodology update: (1)
check standards/laws for current version and applicability; (2) search
for independent replications, systematic reviews, conflicts,
corrections, and retractions; (3) reassess maturity without equating
recency with quality; (4) run affected rule and claim-grammar tests;
(5) version the rule snapshot and preserve the prior one.

When individual studies are used to characterize a wider literature, apply a bounded hierarchy check
for an eligible systematic review or meta-analysis before delivery. Record the check and either include
the synthesis with its context, heterogeneity, and transferability limits, or state that none was found
within the bounded search and narrow the claim to the individual studies. Do not describe a literature
as mixed, settled, or absent from selected individual studies alone; this gate does not authorize an
exhaustive or broad reference hunt.

## Source-use rule (mirrors SKILL.md "연구 근거 활용")

- Cite a principle from this file only when it genuinely supports the
  recommendation being made — do not attach a citation to make a
  heuristic sound more rigorous than it is.
- User-facing phrasing stays in plain Korean. Use "연구 가이드에서
  공통적으로 권장되는 원칙" only when 2+ genuinely independent entries
  above (not the same guidance published twice, as with B3's journal
  article and conference abstract) support the same relevant
  principle; if only one entry supports it, say "한 연구 가이드에서
  권장하는 원칙" (singular) instead — do not imply broader consensus
  than the listed sources actually establish. Unsupported heuristics
  stay "현재 상황에서 권하는 현실적인 기본안" — this file is for
  FieldPilot's internal reasoning, not for dumping citations or class
  labels (A/B/C/D/E) into the conversation.
- Do not present a class B (general research-method, often
  clinical/RCT) finding as if it were class A (direct
  startup/entrepreneurship) evidence, and do not present class D
  (practitioner opinion) with class A/B/C authority.
- Domain-specific numeric thresholds or sample-size figures from
  class B sources (e.g. RCT/medical feasibility sample sizes,
  statistical cutoffs, the "5–20 participants" or "10/20 users" figures
  in B3) never transfer directly to a general B2B market-validation
  pilot — only the structural/procedural principle does.
- Class E frameworks are FieldPilot's own synthesis — never describe
  them as directly research-validated; see the "E" section above.
