# Report actionability — report-actionability-01 (trial)

Use for substantial company/industry/category reports, including a user-specified
outline. This adds purpose-matched synthesis to Route B; it does not turn every
report into a startup consultation. Preserve the 19 invariants, broker reuse,
source review and actual report delivery. Do not load this for a narrow fact.

## 1. Keep the requested job and outline

Keep every requested subject, heading and order. A list of headings specifies
organization, not a ban on useful interpretation. Do not replace it with the
builder six-field template or an invented product. Unknown audience, business
role, budget, geography or goal stays unknown, not an assumed manufacturer.

For multiple subjects, cover each requested section for each subject; share
source retrieval where appropriate but not incompatible numbers/conclusions.
Do not infer that two category requests were a single historical conversation.
Keep smartwatches distinct from all wearables, and wireless earphones distinct
from TWS-only data; disclose a subset used as proxy instead of relabeling it.
These examples illustrate category scope, not universal sector-specific advice.

Retain the existing buyer-opening ideas briefly before the outline when allowed.
If the user explicitly requires only the supplied headings, place the same
meanings inside them: scope early, conclusion/reasons/next check in the last
relevant section. Do not add a compulsory seventh heading or duplicate summary.
Facts-only, no-advice, supplied-source-only and explicit length restrictions win.

## 2. Connect evidence to useful implications

Within applicable sections connect: finding + source -> what it changes for this
question -> a supported implication or clearly labeled hypothesis. Use short
sentences, not repeated mini-frameworks. Put a missing denominator/cost/region
where it affects interpretation. Keep material counterevidence visible.

For a six-section report, these are reasoning checks, not extra output headings:

| Requested section | Preserve the evidence and interpret it |
|---|---|
| Market Size | Define category, geography, period, revenue versus shipments, actual versus forecast. Growth is not proof of reachable demand or profit. |
| Customer Analysis | Needs, situations, segments, user/payer where relevant, switching reasons. Public anecdotes are not this product's tested customers. |
| Competitor Analysis | Compare relevant named direct/substitute choices and strengths/limits. Distinguish existing market alternatives from proposed strategic options. |
| Market Trends | Separate observed adoption from announcements/promotion. Explain the consequence and uncertainty, not a list of buzzwords. |
| Profitability | Specify whose profit: brand/manufacturer/distributor/service, when material. Retail price, BOM and company-wide margin do not establish category or entrant margin. State cost gaps; any scenario inputs are labeled assumptions, never measured profitability. |
| Entry Barriers | Separate barriers by role and jurisdiction. Compare supported ways to respond, including partnership, narrower scope, deferral or no entry, without claiming an easy workaround. |

Reuse adequate evidence. New investigation targets gaps that could change the
answer. Concision removes duplicate prose and methodology, not the reasoning
that connects a conclusion to evidence or distinguishes viable options.

## 3. Synthesize without inventing the user's decision

When the job calls for a recommendation, compare genuinely distinct feasible
options supported by the report. For each material option explain its rationale,
trade-off/prerequisite and what would change its priority. Select the strongest
bounded recommendation and one immediate action/check when criteria support it.
Do not invent a fixed number of options or assert that a hypothesis is a proven
market gap. HOLD or staying with the current approach can be the best choice.

For general market understanding with no declared personal decision, provide
brief conditional implications from the research, not a personal GO/NO-GO or
shopping list. Explain how the preferred direction depends on the decision-maker's
role/constraints. Do accessible checks now; missing user-only criteria may yield
one nonblocking end question under QUESTION_GATE. Do not force interviews,
spending, entry, product development or buying a device on the user.

For facts-only or strict supplied-source tasks, omit unsolicited strategic advice.
Do not silently add external context to repair gaps in supplied sources.
Recommendations that are allowed remain separate from source-derived findings.

## 4. Completion check (content, not just headings)

Before delivering, check each subject independently:
- every requested section and its material scope/unknowns is represented;
- claims have appropriate source support; access limits are honest;
- implications answer this task rather than repeat category descriptions;
- where advice is appropriate, alternatives, selection rationale, trade-offs,
  reversal conditions and the first action/check are understandable;
- no invented user, market numbers, successful entry, margin or demand;
- reading burden is not inflated by duplicate summaries or internal audit text.

If the check fails, revise synthesis first using the evidence already obtained;
research more only for a decision-relevant evidence gap. Preserve report-file
requirements and honest file-tool fallback. A shorter answer, many citations,
file existence or a high average score cannot compensate for failed core advice.
